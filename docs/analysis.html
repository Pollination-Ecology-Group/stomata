Executive Summary

Following full batch deep-learning inference across all 3,253 micrographs on the CESNET GPU cluster, we performed preliminary statistical analysis of the extracted stomatal traits.

A critical consideration in this analysis is the hierarchical nested structure of the experimental system:

    Plant["Random Level 2:\nIndividual Plant (N = 540 plants nested in population)"]

To account for non-independence, correlated error structures, and unequal sample sizes across natural populations, all key traits are evaluated using Linear Mixed-Effects Models (LMM) with Satterthwaite approximations for degrees of freedom:

\[\text{Trait}_{ijkl} = \mu + \text{Cytotype}_i + \text{LeafSide}_j + (\text{Cytotype} \times \text{LeafSide})_{ij} + u_{\text{population}(k)} + v_{\text{plant}(l \subset k)} + \epsilon_{ijkl}\]

where \(u_{\text{population}} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2_{\text{pop}})\) and \(v_{\text{plant}} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2_{\text{plant}})\).

1. Stoma Length Analysis (Cell Size Proxy)

Stoma length (guard cell length along the major axis) is the classical morphological proxy for genome size and cell enlargement (gigas effect).

NoteKey Biological Insights on Stoma Length
  1. Clear Ploidy Separation (\(p < 0.001\)): Diploids (ory_2x: \(18.28\) µm, nat_2x: \(18.56\) µm) are significantly smaller than natural tetraploids (nat_4x: \(20.30\) µm), which in turn are substantially smaller than newly formed synthetic neotetraploids (neo_4x: \(23.52\) µm).
  2. Neopolyploid Hyper-Expansion vs. Natural Downsizing: neo_4x demonstrates an unmitigated gigas effect (+26.7% expansion over nat_2x). In contrast, established wild nat_4x exhibits an intermediate size (+9.4% over nat_2x), suggesting evolutionary cell size optimization or genome downsizing following polyploidization.
  3. Leaf-Side Modulation (\(p < 0.001\)): Across all cytotypes, adaxial (upper surface) stomata are systematically \(0.35 - 0.86\) µm longer than abaxial stomata.

NoteKey Biological Insights on Stomatal Density
  1. Strong Size–Density Compensation: As cell size increases from diploid to tetraploid, stomatal density drops sharply (\(F = 58.4, p < 2.2 \times 10^{-16}\)). neo_4x has the lowest density (\(157.1\) on abaxial, \(133.0\) on adaxial), whereas ory_2x and nat_2x reach \(240 - 280\) stomata/mm².
  2. Surface Specialization (Stomata on Both Surfaces): All cytotypes produce functional stomata on both the upper and lower leaf surfaces, with consistently higher stomatal density on the lower (abaxial) surface than the upper (adaxial) surface (\(F = 573.6, p < 2.2 \times 10^{-16}\)).

6. Biophysical Traits: Maximum Stomatal Conductance (\(g_{s\max}\)) & Stomatal Pore Area Index (SPI)

Using the biophysical formulation of Franks & Farquhar (2001) and Sack & Buckley (2016), theoretical maximum stomatal conductance to water vapor (\(g_{s\max}\), \(\text{mol m}^{-2} \text{s}^{-1}\)) integrates stomatal density (\(D\)), guard cell depth (\(l \approx \text{guard cell width}\)), and maximum pore aperture area (\(a_{\max}\)):

\[g_{s\max} = \frac{d}{v} \cdot \frac{D \cdot a_{\max}}{l + \frac{\pi}{2}\sqrt{a_{\max}/\pi}}\]

where \(d = 2.49 \times 10^{-5} \ \text{m}^2 \text{s}^{-1}\) is water vapor diffusivity in air and \(v = 0.02446 \ \text{m}^3 \text{mol}^{-1}\) is molar volume at 25°C.

NoteEcophysiological Insights on Conductance & SPI
  1. Neo-tetraploid Gas-Exchange Bottleneck (\(p < 0.001\)): Synthetic neo-tetraploids (neo_4x) exhibit significantly lower maximum conductance (\(g_{s\max} \approx 0.72\) on abaxial, \(0.60 \ \text{mol m}^{-2} \text{s}^{-1}\) on adaxial) than all other cytotypes. The steep reduction in stomatal density (\(>35\%\)) is not compensated by larger pore dimensions.
  2. Evolutionary Optimization in Natural Tetraploids: In contrast, natural tetraploids (nat_4x) recover high conductance (\(0.90 \ \text{mol m}^{-2} \text{s}^{-1}\) abaxial), closely matching diploid ancestors (nat_2x: \(0.87\), ory_2x: \(0.99\)). Natural selection following whole-genome duplication has evidently tuned the balance of cell size and packing density to optimize gas-exchange capacity.

To account for non-independence, correlated error structures, and unequal sample sizes across natural populations, all key traits are evaluated using **Linear Mixed-Effects Models (LMM)** with Satterthwaite approximations for degrees of freedom:

$$\text{Trait}_{ijkl} = \mu + \text{Cytotype}_i + \text{LeafSide}_j + (\text{Cytotype} \times \text{LeafSide})_{ij} + u_{\text{population}(k)} + v_{\text{plant}(l \subset k)} + \epsilon_{ijkl}$$

where $u_{\text{population}} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2_{\text{pop}})$ and $v_{\text{plant}} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2_{\text{plant}})$.

## 6. Biophysical Traits: Maximum Stomatal Conductance ($g_{s\max}$) & Stomatal Pore Area Index (SPI)

Using the biophysical formulation of Franks & Farquhar (2001) and Sack & Buckley (2016), theoretical maximum stomatal conductance to water vapor ($g_{s\max}$, $\text{mol m}^{-2} \text{s}^{-1}$) integrates stomatal density ($D$), guard cell depth ($l \approx \text{guard cell width}$), and maximum pore aperture area ($a_{\max}$):

$$g_{s\max} = \frac{d}{v} \cdot \frac{D \cdot a_{\max}}{l + \frac{\pi}{2}\sqrt{a_{\max}/\pi}}$$

where $d = 2.49 \times 10^{-5} \ \text{m}^2 \text{s}^{-1}$ is water vapor diffusivity in air and $v = 0.02446 \ \text{m}^3 \text{mol}^{-1}$ is molar volume at 25°C.

## 7. Cell-Level Hierarchical Variance Decomposition ($N = 51,460$ Stomata)

To test whether newly formed polyploids exhibit elevated **cell-to-cell developmental noise** (fluctuating asymmetry or developmental instability), we fit a fully nested hierarchical mixed-effects model directly to all $51,460$ individually measured ploidy-suitable stomata:

$$\text{Length}_{ijklm} = \mu + \text{Cytotype}_i \times \text{LeafSide}_j + u_{\text{pop}(k)} + v_{\text{plant}(l \subset k)} + w_{\text{photo}(m \subset l)} + \epsilon_{ijklm}$$

```{r cell-level-model}
# Load full cell-level dataset if available
cell_file <- "data/stomata_traits_all_3253_stomata.csv"
if (file.exists(cell_file)) {
  df_cells <- read.csv(cell_file, stringsAsFactors = FALSE) %>%
    filter(is_suitable_for_ploidy_sizing == TRUE | is_suitable_for_ploidy_sizing == "True") %>%
    mutate(
      cytotype = factor(cytotype, levels = c("ory_2x", "nat_2x", "nat_4x", "neo_4x")),
      leaf_side = factor(leaf_side, levels = c("abaxial", "adaxial")),
      population = factor(population),
      plant_ID = factor(plant_ID),
      image_name = factor(image_name)
    )

  m_cell <- lmer(
    stoma_major_um ~ cytotype * leaf_side + (1 | population) + (1 | population:plant_ID) + (1 | population:plant_ID:image_name),
    data = df_cells
  )

  # Extract variance components
  vc <- as.data.frame(VarCorr(m_cell)) %>%
    select(grp, vcov, sdcor) %>%
    rename(Level = grp, Variance = vcov, StdDev = sdcor)

  kable(vc, digits = 3, caption = "Variance Component Decomposition across Hierarchical Levels for Stoma Length") %>%
    kable_styling(bootstrap_options = c("striped", "hover", "condensed"))
}
```

## Conclusions

1. **Enhanced Trait Extraction Engine:**
   - The retrained YOLOv11 segmentation model (incorporating 175 background negative controls and active-learning crops) achieved **mAP50 of 0.972** and **Precision of 0.944**, successfully eliminating false detections on plant cuticle.

2. **Clear Evolutionary Differentiation:**
   - **Diploid Baseline:** `ory_2x` ($18.99$ µm) and `nat_2x` ($19.20$ µm) maintain high density and small, tightly regulated stomata.
   - **Neopolyploid Hyper-Expansion:** `neo_4x` ($24.52$ µm) displays massive cellular enlargement accompanied by a severe drop in stomatal density, leading to a theoretical conductance bottleneck ($g_{s\max} = 0.718$).
   - **Post-Polyploid Evolutionary Downsizing:** Established wild tetraploids (`nat_4x`, $20.95$ µm) demonstrate evolutionary downsizing and density recovery, restoring high theoretical conductance ($g_{s\max} = 0.902$).
